01 — The basics

Alberta's Dower Act

Your name doesn't have to be on the title for the law to matter.

Where the Act applies, a married spouse who is not the registered owner may have specific rights relating to a qualifying family home.

“Not being on title does not automatically mean you have no rights.”
02 — Who may be protected

Three questions help establish whether the Act applies.

01

Are you legally married?

Dower rights are specific to legally married spouses.

02

Does one spouse own the property?

The spouse claiming dower rights may not be listed as an owner on title.

03

Is it a qualifying homestead?

The property must meet the legal requirements of the Act. Residence is an important part of determining whether it applies.

03 — Know the limits

The Dower Act does not apply to every situation.

Other laws may provide rights even where the Dower Act does not.

Common-law relationshipsLiving together without legal marriage does not, by itself, create dower rights under the Dower Act.
DivorceDivorce can affect whether the Dower Act continues to apply. Other legal rights may remain.
Joint ownershipIf both spouses are registered owners, the legal situation is different because both already have an ownership interest.
Non-homestead propertyNot every property owned by a married spouse is automatically protected.
04 — Before you sign

Spousal consent

Stop. Read. Ask. Understand.

When the Dower Act applies, the owner-spouse generally needs the required consent of the other spouse before making certain dispositions of the homestead.

05 — A key concept

Life estate

A legal interest that may matter after a spouse dies.

The Dower Act can provide a surviving spouse with a life estate in a qualifying homestead in circumstances established by the Act. A life estate can give a person a right to occupy and use property during their lifetime.

06 — If something goes wrong

Missing dower requirements do not have a one-size-fits-all outcome.

A potential issue can arise when a spouse deals with a qualifying homestead without obtaining required consent. The consequences depend on the facts and the transaction.

✓ Type of transaction

✓ Whether consent was obtained

✓ Documents that were signed

✓ Whether an affidavit was provided

✓ What other parties knew

Discovered before closing? Address missing dower requirements before the transaction proceeds. Discovered after closing? Get legal advice promptly. Do not assume the outcome.
